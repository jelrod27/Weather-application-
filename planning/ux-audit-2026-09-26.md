# 16-Bit Weather: UX comparison after the six-PR batch and stabilization

**Reviewed:** September 26, 2026 · **Production:** https://www.16bitweather.co · **Audience:** everyday users first, with depth for enthusiasts.

**Baseline:** [September 25 audit](./ux-audit-2026-09-25.md). **Delivered work:** UX PRs #627–632 and stabilization PR #633. PR #633 was merged, and its production deployment reported success before this walkthrough. Merge commit: `9268e34c9ccf2c492a588b5b0b20d2538c73c976`.

## Assessment

The site now helps people make decisions with its data. The forecast summarizes the next few hours, the outdoor planner exposes useful tradeoffs, the radar journey preserves context, and Stargazer gives beginners a time, direction and equipment choice. These are substantial improvements over the disconnected reference tools reviewed yesterday.

**Overall assessment: 7.8/10 now, compared with a retrospective 5.2/10 for the baseline.** This is an expert walkthrough, not a user study. Yesterday's report did not assign numerical grades; both scores below use the same rubric applied now to the documented before/after evidence. The difference is not a measured percentage increase in usability, conversion or task completion.

No critical regression caused by these seven PRs was established. That is not a claim that the site is regression-free. The signed-in dashboard performed worse during this visit: two of four saved-location forecasts remained unavailable after one retry each. That is a priority reliability finding whose cause and relationship to the batch remain unverified. Several older trust and discovery issues also remain.

## Grading rubric

Ratings are rounded judgments on a 0–10 scale: **3** means a key task is unreliable or misleading; **5** means useful information exists but users must reconcile it themselves; **7** means useful tasks work with noticeable gaps; **9** means clear, coherent and dependable across the sampled normal and recovery paths. A 10 would require stronger evidence than this walkthrough provides. Weights prioritize everyday decisions. Accessibility is explicitly excluded from this score and reserved for its own assessment.

## What the live data now lets a visitor do

**Plan an outing.** London's two-hour comparisons separated lower precipitation chance (11 AM–1 PM, peak 0%), lighter wind (6–8 AM, up to 6 mph), and temperatures nearer 68°F (5–7 PM, but peak precipitation chance 53%). The last choice was not presented as universally “best.” Users can see the tradeoff, examine the underlying hours, then open radar without starting over.

There is a small explanation opportunity: View hourly readings initially selects the start boundary. At 11 AM that reading showed 2% for the hour *ending* at 11 AM, while the 11 AM–1 PM outing correctly used the following interval readings and showed 0%. The details explain the interval convention, but aligning the entry point or emphasizing the covered period would reduce apparent disagreement. This is not evidence of a scoring error.

**Investigate a warning.** The selected warning now determines the radar area and overlay. For a warning with no polygon, the page explicitly refuses to imply an affected area and offers the actual official alert. The filter-preserving return is useful. Some internal terminology remains prominent: “canonical store,” “Warning Event,” “Source Message,” “Delivery,” and “Protected Place” are harder to understand than ordinary weather language.

**Try stargazing.** For a New York 10–11 PM interval, naked-eye targets included Moon and Saturn with southeast directions and altitude. Saturn's appearance guidance explains that rings need a telescope. Heavy cloud was visible as 100% coverage; target positions were conditional on skies clearing. This is much more useful to a first-time visitor than an astrophotography score alone.

**Learn from current weather.** The connected three-step illustrations teach cloud shape, storm structure and radar interpretation. However, a visitor starting at Education Hub, Cloud Atlas or Weather Systems receives no direct link to `/education/weather-skills` in the inspected rendered content. The strongest new teaching surface is discoverable from forecast/radar, but insufficiently promoted in the learning section itself.

## Issues and possible regressions, prioritized

### 1. Diagnose saved-location weather failures

**Observed degradation; cause unknown.** Two of four saved forecasts loaded and two remained unavailable after one Retry each. Console output reported failed dashboard weather fetches. Yesterday's read-only account walkthrough reported saved forecasts loading. This may be provider availability, caching, specific saved coordinates, request handling or a code change; this audit did not establish which.

Next work should inspect the matching server failures and compare a failing card's request with the same place's main forecast. Keep the last successful data visibly dated where appropriate, distinguish unavailable from empty, and verify retries recover. Do not infer a universal 50% failure rate from this one account and visit. No private location details are retained here.

### 2. Finish consistency and freshness work

- **Existing radar gap:** show elapsed age on the newest frame as well as historical frames. “Latest available” can already be several minutes old. The new radar lesson asks users to check age, so the live control should support it.
- **Newly identified legacy Moon-card problem:** the main forecast displayed approximately the same 6:09 AM moonset for London and New York, while Stargazer showed a different local New York moonset. Source inspection confirmed that `calculateMoonPhase()` in `lib/weather/weather-utils.ts` estimates moonset from lunar age without coordinates or a viewed-location zone. The file predates this batch (last change July 17). Reuse the location-aware astronomy calculation or clearly remove/qualify that approximation. Do not leave precise-looking local Moon times from two incompatible methods.
- **Existing travel gap:** replace the overall CLEAR message when the same row highlights high winds or dense fog. Distinguish the route summary from its worst stretch, using named places and times. The completed flight result also deserves a consistency check against its national airport board when their scores differ; this audit did not establish that different scoring scopes constitute a calculation defect.

### 3. Make the visual learning work discoverable

Put the illustrated lessons in Education Hub's Start Here and beside the related atlas/front entries. A user asking “what cloud is that?” should see a visual comparison early, not only taxonomy counts and long descriptions. Photos or curated faithful examples, look-alike comparisons and a short identification path remain the largest unfulfilled learning opportunity from the original audit.

### 4. Finish account and editorial trust details

Use a sign-up heading on the sign-up state; show authentication success only after a verified result; replace the saved-location empty flash with loading. Finish the featured blog title and continue editorial normalization of source-heavy news. Clarify or retire the persistently unavailable GFS surface until a usable model path is available. Radar Diagnostic still combines current RainViewer build metadata with obsolete NEXRAD verification instructions.

### 5. Polish continuity and deeper-tool presentation

- London remains at the same coordinates through the connected journey, but reverse geocoding renames it **City of Westminster** on Hourly/Radar and the return forecast. Preserve the user's selected display name where possible. This is a newly observed continuity issue, not a wrong-coordinate finding.
- On the 390px Hourly screen, **Read the sky** wraps into three short lines. The navigation works but is less tidy than the rest of the new UI.
- Stargazer's **Upcoming Launches** list included already successful launches from September 25/26. Separate recent launches from future events.
- The traditional deep-sky reference remains denser and more optimistic about generic visibility than the new beginner view. Continue distinguishing catalog facts from tonight's conditions.

## Limits and next validation

This was the same broad expert UI/UX audit, expanded for shipped features. It is not a new load test, performance benchmark, forecast-accuracy study, security sweep or full accessibility audit. No new Lighthouse score or claimed load-time improvement is inferred from screenshots or successful navigation. Historical data and different live weather prevent a controlled numerical comparison of forecasts.

No account creation, email delivery, OAuth completion, password change/reset delivery, valid-token subscription mutation, browser notification permission, geolocation permission, social posting, community upload or live aircraft selection was exercised. No real-world observing result or warning delivery is claimed. Every catalog/article URL was checked for availability, but not every article fact, map combination, theme, device or alternate-unit preference was retested.

Accessibility remains reserved for a focused pass as requested. The previously repaired learning-card keyboard interaction was spot-checked; broader contrast, labels, screen-reader behavior and keyboard coverage remain outside this grade.

After the reliability and remaining trust fixes, ask everyday visitors to perform five uncoached tasks: choose an outdoor hour, decide whether a warning affects their place, use radar and return, identify a cloud, and choose something to look for tonight. Measure completion, incorrect conclusions and confidence. The cloud-identification task is the one this walkthrough suggests will still struggle most.

## Closeout

Only audit documentation, checklists and public evidence were created. No application code or persistent account data was changed. No commit, push or PR was created. The unrelated `.agents/` directory and prototype work were preserved. The temporary signed-in audit tab was closed and the responsive browser viewport restored.

The live HTTP/browser checks above were run. Unit, build, lint and local CI suites were not rerun because there were no application changes; their earlier PR results are not being presented as fresh audit results. The highest-priority follow-up is to diagnose the two saved-forecast failures, followed by consistent freshness/Moon/travel semantics and discovery of the illustrated lessons.
