## Rearview

The defining story of the Oct. 18–25 interval was a sustained convective outbreak across the Desert Southwest — a region where tornado warnings are rare enough that 23 of them across seven days constitutes a significant signal.

Among the 469 polygon warnings issued during the period (330 severe thunderstorm, 71 flash flood, 23 tornado, 45 marine), the tornado warnings were concentrated almost entirely in Arizona and New Mexico. The Flagstaff WFO (FGZ) and El Paso WFO (EPZ) issued the bulk of them, with the Albuquerque WFO (ABQ) contributing several more. On the evening of Oct. 22 local time, warnings fired across Apache, Navajo, Yavapai, Cibola, McKinley, and Sandoval counties in a span of roughly four hours — a geographic spread that runs from the Arizona high country into central New Mexico.

Two tornadoes were confirmed in SPC preliminary reports. The first, rated EF1, touched down southeast of Ponderosa Park in Yavapai County, Arizona, around 7:30 a.m. local time on Oct. 21, tracking north-northeast across Walker Road for approximately 8 miles with estimated peak winds of 105 mph. The second, also EF1, struck the Heber-Overgaard area in Navajo County, Arizona, during the early morning hours of Oct. 22 local time — an 11-minute track that injured one person and produced peak winds estimated at 110 mph.

![GOES-16 visible-band view of the contiguous US.](https://cdn.star.nesdis.noaa.gov/GOES16/ABI/CONUS/02/1250x750.jpg)
*NOAA NESDIS*

The broader SPC preliminary report total for the period reached 108 events across seven days, with 76 hail reports and 30 wind reports. Arizona accounted for 72 of those reports, New Mexico 24. Hail reached 1.25 inches near Pirtleville in Cochise County, Arizona, on the evening of Oct. 19 local time, and a separate storm produced a photographed 2-inch stone east of Mesa in Maricopa County just after midnight local time Oct. 21. Wind reports included a mesonet-measured gust of 77 mph near Rodeo, New Mexico, on the evening of Oct. 19, and a 72 mph gust at Flagstaff Airport on the afternoon of Oct. 21.

![The record 7.9-inch hailstone that fell in Vivian, South Dakota on July 23, 2010 — the largest U.S. hailstone on record.](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Record%20hailstone%20Vivian,%20SD.jpg?width=1280)
*NWS Aberdeen, SD / NOAA*

On the seismic side, the week produced eight significant earthquakes (USGS significance threshold exceeded). The most consequential cluster hit near Vanuatu: a M6.6 and M6.3 struck within seconds of each other at 04:20 UTC on Oct. 20, both shallow (10–16 km depth) and both generating tsunami alerts. A second Vanuatu sequence followed on Oct. 24 — a M6.9 at 10 km depth, tsunami-alerting, accompanied by a M6.3 minutes later. Separately, a M7.0 struck 191 km ENE of Nagqu, China, on Oct. 22 at a depth of 18 km, and a M6.5 hit the Bonin Islands region on Oct. 25 at 492 km depth — deep enough that tsunami risk was limited despite the magnitude.

![Seismic wave paths through Earth after an earthquake.](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Earthquake_wave_paths.svg?width=1280)

Space weather was unremarkable. The week's peak planetary Kp reached 4.67 among observed blocks — below the G1 storm threshold of 5. A single M1.7 solar flare was recorded at 09:03 UTC on Oct. 19. No geomagnetic storm conditions were observed in the 56 three-hour blocks of Kp data.

M4.5+ earthquake data was unavailable due to a server error; the broader magnitude picture for the week is incomplete.

## The Takeaway

- The Arizona–New Mexico tornado outbreak of Oct. 21–25 is worth tracking through final NWS storm data publications, since two EF1 confirmations and 23 warnings across a region with minimal tornado climatology suggest the event will draw post-season analysis.
- The Vanuatu seismic sequences of Oct. 20 and Oct. 24 — two separate doublets, both shallow, both tsunami-alerting — are a reminder that the New Hebrides subduction zone can produce rapid-succession large events; regional tsunami preparedness protocols in the southwest Pacific remain relevant.
- Chicago-area residents should watch the Oct. 29–31 window, where precipitation probability trends upward each day and the modeled accumulation, while modest, represents the most consistent multi-day wet signal in the domestic 7-day outlook.
