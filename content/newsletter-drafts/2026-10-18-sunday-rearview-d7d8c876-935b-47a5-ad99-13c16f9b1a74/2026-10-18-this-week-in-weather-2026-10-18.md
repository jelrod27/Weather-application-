## Rearview

North Carolina absorbed the week's most concentrated tornado activity on the afternoon of October 11. Between roughly 10:14 a.m. and 1:38 p.m. EDT, SPC preliminary reports logged tornado touchdowns across Montgomery, Cumberland, Chatham, Columbus, Wake, Johnston, Pender, Sampson, and Duplin counties. The NWS Raleigh office confirmed an EF-2 near Wallace in Duplin County — peak winds estimated at 115–125 mph, path length 24.43 miles, width approximately 150 yards. A separate EF-2 touched down near Bonnie Doone in Cumberland County with a peak wind survey of 115 mph along a short quarter-mile section near a Walmart and Sam's Club on the south side of Fayetteville. Shorter-track EF-1s were confirmed near Carpenter in Wake County, Linden in Cumberland County, and Jordan Lake in Chatham County. The preliminary SPC log records 20 tornado reports on October 11 alone, all of them in North Carolina — 56 of the 297 total reports across the week's coverage came from that state.

The broader warning count for the October 11–18 interval reached 363 polygon warnings across all five tracked types: 201 severe thunderstorm, 78 flash flood, 40 tornado, 43 marine, and 1 extreme wind. A second tornado-warning cluster appeared October 14–15, with the WFO Lubbock office issuing four tornado warnings between roughly 5:06 p.m. and 6:28 p.m. CDT on October 14 across Garza, Kent, Dickens, Stonewall, and King counties in the Texas South Plains. The WFO Norman office followed with two tornado warnings in Tillman County, Oklahoma and adjacent Wichita and Wilbarger counties in Texas just after 8:16 p.m. CDT. WFO Indianapolis then issued two tornado warnings in Knox and Daviess counties, Indiana around 7:07 p.m. CDT on October 15.

Total SPC preliminary reports across the interval reached 297, led by 247 wind reports and 27 tornado reports. Missouri led all states with 93 reports; North Carolina was second at 56.

On the seismic side, 114 M4.5-or-greater earthquakes were recorded globally during the interval. The largest among available observations was a M6.9 at 135 km depth, 76 km northeast of Ozernovskiy on the Kamchatka Peninsula, at 3:35 a.m. UTC on October 16. Two M6.7–6.8 events struck the central Mid-Atlantic Ridge on October 13 — the M6.8 at 12:23 UTC carried a tsunami flag, though the remote mid-ocean location limits coastal exposure. A M6.1 at shallow depth (11 km) struck 17 km west of Río Grande, Panama on October 12.

Geomagnetic activity remained quiet through most of the interval. The peak observed Kp among available three-hour blocks was 3.67, recorded at 18:00 UTC on October 16 — well below the Kp 5 threshold for even minor storm classification. An M1.4 solar flare was recorded at 15:42 UTC on October 16; no significant geomagnetic follow-on is evident in the subsequent Kp readings, which dropped to 2.33 or below within three hours.

## Looking Ahead

- San Francisco's October 21 precipitation signal — 0.40 inches at 56% probability — is worth tracking for the Bay Area's first meaningful rain of the season; check updated model runs Monday and Tuesday for confidence trends.
- The M6.8 Mid-Atlantic Ridge event on October 13 carried a tsunami flag; review PTWC post-event communications if you work in coastal emergency management, as preliminary tsunami flags on remote ridge events are often canceled but warrant documentation.
- The South Plains tornado warning cluster on October 14–15 and the North Carolina outbreak on October 11 both occurred during what is historically a secondary peak in the Southern Plains severe weather calendar — the October window warrants the same operational awareness as spring.
