## Rearview

The most concentrated severe weather of the past week hit Southern California on Thursday, November 19, when a convective line swept through Los Angeles County between roughly 8 a.m. and 10:30 a.m. local time. Among available SPC preliminary reports, 20 of 29 total events were logged in California across the full interval. The November 19 cluster alone generated wind reports from Magic Mountain Truck Trail (69 mph), Camp Nine near San Fernando (68 mph), and multiple sites in Santa Barbara and San Luis Obispo counties, plus a storm survey finding that a diffuse circulation embedded in the line produced sporadic tree damage across Inglewood and Brentwood. Two tornado warnings were issued for San Benito and Merced counties on November 20, and a separate warning had been issued for Santa Cruz County on the evening of November 16 — all three from the WFO MTR office.

South Florida had its own convective sequence late Saturday night into early Sunday morning. WFO MLB issued six tornado warnings between 11:57 p.m. local time Saturday and 11:18 p.m. local time Saturday through approximately 10:08 p.m. local time Saturday across Palm Beach, Okeechobee, Indian River, and St. Lucie counties, with WFO MFL covering Palm Beach. A quarter-inch hail report from Hollywood, Broward County, was logged early Thursday morning local time (04:10 UTC November 18), associated with a separate overnight severe thunderstorm. The full warning dataset across the interval recorded 255 polygon warnings: 186 marine warnings, 43 flash flood warnings, 16 severe thunderstorm warnings, and 10 tornado warnings.

Seismically, the week's largest event was a M6.8 at 132 km depth, 105 km northeast of Lospalos, Timor Leste, at 9:42 a.m. local time Saturday, November 21 — no tsunami was generated. A M6.7 near Carhuamayo, Peru (107 km depth) earlier that same day did trigger a tsunami alert, though the shallow-origin hazard was moderated by depth. A M6.6 struck 149 km west-southwest of Tual, Indonesia on Monday, November 16 at 68 km depth. Among available USGS significant-event records, seven events met the significance threshold across the interval; 99 earthquakes of M4.5 or greater were catalogued.

## Roadmap

The dominant story for the coming week is a cold airmass settling over the central and eastern United States behind a front that clears the Ohio and Tennessee Valleys Sunday night into Monday, followed by a more significant coastal storm system that arrives around Thanksgiving. Travelers heading anywhere east of the Rockies this week should be watching both the front's immediate cold and a developing storm that the WPC is flagging as a potential major event for the East Coast by Wednesday through Thursday.

![Polar and subtropical jet stream configuration.](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Jetstreamconfig.jpg?width=1280)
*NOAA*

[NOAA's short-range forecast discussion](https://www.wpc.ncep.noaa.gov/discussions/pmdspdbody.txt) (issued 2:26 p.m. EST Sunday, November 22; valid through 7 p.m. EST Tuesday, November 24) describes a coastal low tracking through the Gulf of Maine on Monday, bringing moderate to locally heavy rain to New England overnight into Monday morning. Behind it, a strong cold front pushes through the Southeast and Mid-Atlantic, with lake-effect snow developing in the Upper Peninsula of Michigan and northern Wisconsin by Monday. The first freeze of the season is expected across parts of west and north Texas, with hard freeze conditions possible in Oklahoma by Tuesday night. A wintry mix is possible across New Mexico and the southern High Plains Monday night into Tuesday.

[NOAA's extended forecast discussion](https://www.wpc.ncep.noaa.gov/discussions/pmdepdbody.txt) (issued 3:00 a.m. EST Sunday, November 22; valid Wednesday, November 25 through Sunday, November 29) describes two mid-level disturbances phasing over the eastern half of the country, producing what the WPC characterizes as "hazardous weather" across the East Coast through the Thanksgiving holiday. Heavy rain and winds gusting above 40 knots are expected to reach the Southeast and Mid-Atlantic coasts by Wednesday, with a slight risk of excessive rainfall along the Carolina coast east of I-95. The system intensifies Thursday, spreading heavy rain and damaging winds into the I-95 corridor. Some snowfall is possible over the Central Appalachians, Lower Great Lakes, and interior New England on Thanksgiving and into Friday. The WPC explicitly flags this as a storm travelers should monitor closely.
