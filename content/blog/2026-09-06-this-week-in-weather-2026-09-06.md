## Roadmap

The pattern shaping the week of September 7–13 is a heat-dominated south and a moisture-starved northeast, with the Midwest carrying most of the week's convective load. A persistent upper-level ridge centered over Texas is the controlling feature — it's keeping Dallas at an average high of 100°F through the period and suppressing precipitation probability to just 11% even at peak (September 10). That ridge is not static, however. A shortwave trough digging into the northern Plains mid-week is the primary disturbance to watch, and its track will determine how much severe weather potential materializes east of the Mississippi.

Total weekly precipitation for the Chicago area is projected at 0.41 inches, nearly all of it concentrated around that Tuesday window.

**Southeast.** Atlanta averages 91°F through the period, with the peak precipitation probability — 51% — arriving on September 11, a day after the Midwest event. That timing is consistent with the shortwave's eastward progression and the attendant surface low tracking across the Tennessee Valley. The week's total precip for Atlanta is 0.26 inches, modest but not negligible given the heat load. Afternoon convection building off elevated terrain to the north is the most likely delivery mechanism.
