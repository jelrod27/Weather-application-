## Rearview

Panama absorbed the week's most consequential seismic sequence. On October 9 at 12:56 p.m. local time, a M7.7 struck 12 km WSW of Pitaloza Arriba — the largest earthquake recorded globally in the past seven days and one of the stronger events in Central America in recent years. Two additional significant earthquakes followed in the same region: a M6.6 14 km west of Río Grande (which carried a tsunami flag) and a M6.0 7 km north of La Tronosa, along with a M5.8 near El Cacao. Four significant earthquakes in a single country within a compressed window is a genuine aftershock sequence, not coincidence.

On the atmospheric side, North Carolina bore the weight of the week's tornado activity. Between 12:52 and 1:24 p.m. EDT on October 11, the Wilmington, Raleigh, and Morehead City forecast offices issued six tornado warnings across Bladen, Columbus, Pender, Johnston, Wake, Sampson, Franklin, and Duplin counties — a tight geographic cluster that suggests a single organized convective system tracking northeast through the coastal plain. The SPC logged 15 tornado reports nationally for the week, with Iowa leading all states at 32 total storm reports.

The week's 618 NWS warnings included 106 flash flood warnings and 132 marine warnings — a distribution that reflects both the North Carolina convective event and likely coastal effects. Iowa's 32 storm reports were concentrated in hail and wind, consistent with fast-moving convective lines rather than discrete supercell activity.

Geomagnetic activity reached G3 (Strong) storm levels on October 10. NOAA's [3-hour Kp observations](https://services.swpc.noaa.gov/products/noaa-planetary-k-index.json) show a peak of 7 for the interval beginning at 15:00 UTC that day. In its [forecast discussion issued October 11 at 12:30 UTC](https://services.swpc.noaa.gov/text/discussion.txt), NOAA attributed the storm to coronal mass ejections (CMEs) from October 7–8, whose arrival was detected at 14:34 UTC on October 10.
