## Rearview

The week's most concentrated severe weather landed on Thursday, November 12, when five tornado warnings were issued across the Mid-Atlantic corridor — Queens (NY), New Castle (DE), and Burlington and Mercer counties (NJ) — all within a roughly three-hour window in the afternoon and early evening local time. Flash flood warnings followed through the overnight hours, covering Monmouth, Ocean, Burlington, Camden, and Bucks counties across New Jersey and southeastern Pennsylvania. The geographic clustering across that corridor points to a single organized system tracking northeast through the coastal plain.

![Wedge tornado near Binger, Oklahoma (1981).](https://www.spc.noaa.gov/faq/tornado/binger.jpg)
*NOAA NSSL archive*

The prior day's setup had already been active. On Wednesday, November 11, an EF-0 tornado touched down 2 miles southwest of Riley in Vigo County, Indiana, at approximately 5:27 PM local time, with a path width of 25 yards and a track of 1.1 miles — enough to damage a barn and an RV. Hail reports from that same system extended from Scott and Rock Island counties in Iowa and Illinois southward into Gibson, Posey, and Warrick counties in southwestern Indiana, with the largest stone measured at 1.75 inches (near Riley, Vigo County). Wind damage reports from that evening extended into Breckinridge, Muhlenberg, and Barren counties in Kentucky.

Across the full reporting interval (November 8–15), validated polygon warnings totaled 114 across all five tracked types: 47 marine warnings, 42 severe thunderstorm warnings, 20 flash flood warnings, and 5 tornado warnings. SPC preliminary reports logged 73 total events among available observations, with Indiana (12 reports) and New Jersey (24 reports) as the leading states by count — a reflection of the two distinct convective episodes on November 11 and November 12.

On the space weather side, a GOES-primary M1.8 X-ray flare was recorded at 21:21 UTC on November 11. The planetary Kp index peaked at 3.33 among the 52 of 56 three-hour blocks with confirmed observations — geomagnetically quiet, well below storm threshold.

One earthquake cleared the USGS significance threshold during the interval: a M6.4 on November 9 at 03:34 UTC, located 30 km west-southwest of Lata in the Solomon Islands, at a shallow depth of 17 km. No tsunami was generated. An additional 119 M4.5-or-greater earthquakes were catalogued globally during the period.

## Roadmap

The WPC short-range discussion (issued 2:18 PM EST Sunday, November 15; valid 00Z Monday, November 16 through 00Z Wednesday, November 18; source: [https://www.wpc.ncep.noaa.gov/discussions/pmdspdbody.txt](https://www.wpc.ncep.noaa.gov/discussions/pmdspdbody.txt)) describes an upper-level trough departing the Northeast while high pressure builds into the Ohio Valley by Monday and pushes into the central Appalachians by Tuesday. That ridge will keep the Southeast largely clear through Tuesday. A separate system over the Northwest moves inland toward the Rockies by Tuesday, bringing rain and higher-elevation snow across the Northern Intermountain Region and the Sierra, where Winter Weather Advisories are in effect through Tuesday. Residual Gulf moisture supports rain and embedded thunderstorms over the Southern Plains through Sunday evening, with showers lingering over the Lower Mississippi Valley into late Monday morning. Easterly Atlantic flow produces scattered showers along the Florida coast and the adjacent Gulf Coast on Tuesday.

The WPC medium-range discussion (issued 1:53 PM EST Sunday, November 15; valid 12Z Wednesday, November 18 through 12Z Sunday, November 22; source: [https://www.wpc.ncep.noaa.gov/discussions/pmdepdbody.txt](https://www.wpc.ncep.noaa.gov/discussions/pmdepdbody.txt)) flags an amplified upper-level pattern: mean troughing over the West, a ridge shifting east. The dominant hazard of the period is a strong upper low and associated atmospheric river targeting California on Wednesday, November 18, and Thursday, November 19 — WPC uses "moderate to heavy rainfall" language for coastal and interior California, with flooding risk noted and heavy snow expected in the Sierra, parts of Utah, and the San Juans. That moisture then spreads across the Intermountain West and into the Plains late in the week. A separate shortwave lifts into the north-central U.S. on Wednesday, November 18, spreading light precipitation across parts of the Central U.S. and the Great Lakes. High winds are expected behind the frontal system across the Plains on Friday, November 20, and Saturday, November 21.

Temperatures through the medium range are forecast above normal across the eastern half of the country under the ridge — the Upper Midwest in the 50s through Wednesday, November 18, with highs in the 60s reaching as far north as the Great Lakes region and 70s through the southern tier later in the week. California and the central Great Basin remain cooler than average under the upper trough.

WPC also flags an uncertain low-pressure signal near and east of Florida late in the week, with some model guidance suggesting a surface low could track north along or near the East Coast next weekend. Confidence is low; the discussion notes the 12Z GFS is an outlier in pulling the low inland into the Southeast while other models keep it offshore.

---

The week handed the Mid-Atlantic two days of November convection that most years would be unremarkable — except that five tornado warnings in a single afternoon across Queens, Delaware, and the Jersey Shore is not a routine November configuration for that corridor. What follows is a pattern that reads almost as a reset: high pressure, clearing skies, and quiet air across the East, while the next significant energy organizes over the Pacific and lines up California for its first meaningful atmospheric river of the season. The transition is clean on paper; the execution, particularly the uncertain low near Florida late next week, is still being negotiated by the models.
