## Rearview

The week's most concentrated severe weather landed Thursday, November 12, when five tornado warnings fired across the Philadelphia WFO and one from New York's OKX office — all within roughly a four-hour window from mid-afternoon into early evening Eastern time. A tornado warning for Queens, NY issued at 2:14 PM EST was followed by warnings for New Castle County, Delaware at 2:13 PM and a sequence for Burlington, Mercer, and Monmouth counties in New Jersey extending through 4:30 PM. Preliminary SPC reports logged 24 severe events in New Jersey and 10 in Delaware that same day, with hail and wind damage concentrated in the coastal plain corridor. Flash flood warnings from the Philadelphia WFO cascaded through the overnight hours for Burlington, Camden, Gloucester, Mercer, Monmouth, and Ocean counties in New Jersey and several Pennsylvania counties including Philadelphia and Montgomery — the latest expiring at 11:00 PM EST Friday morning.

The prior day, Wednesday November 11, produced the week's most geographically dispersed convection. An EF-0 tornado — 85 mph peak winds, 25-yard path width, 1.1-mile track — touched down near Riley in Vigo County, Indiana at approximately 5:27 PM CST, damaging a barn and an RV. Hail reports from that same system ranged across Scott and Rock Island counties in Iowa and Illinois through the afternoon, with the largest stone measured at 1.75 inches near Riley, Indiana in the evening. Wind damage extended into Breckinridge, Muhlenberg, and Barren counties in Kentucky into the early morning hours of November 12.

Across the full reporting interval, IEM polygon data shows 114 warnings of the tracked types — 5 tornado warnings, 42 severe thunderstorm warnings, 20 flash flood warnings, and 47 marine warnings — with SPC preliminary reports totaling 73 storm reports: 5 tornado, 52 hail, and 16 wind entries across Indiana, Iowa, Illinois, Kentucky, New Jersey, Delaware, Pennsylvania, Maryland, California, and Texas. These counts reflect validated observations within the interval; any events outside the archived window are not captured here.

## Roadmap

[NOAA's short-range outlook](https://www.wpc.ncep.noaa.gov/discussions/pmdspdbody.txt), issued Sunday November 15 at 2:18 PM EST and valid through Wednesday November 18, describes a pattern in transition: an upper-level trough exiting the Northeast, high pressure building into the Ohio Valley and Central Appalachians by Monday and Tuesday, and a front pushing inland from the Northwest into the Rockies. The practical result for most of the eastern U.S. is a quiet, dry stretch through Tuesday. The active zones are the Southern Plains — where upper-level energy moving east produces rain and embedded thunderstorms through Sunday evening — and the Northwest and Northern Intermountain Region, where a frontal system brings rain at lower elevations and snow at higher elevations, with Winter Weather Advisories already posted for portions of the Sierra Nevada.

[NOAA's extended outlook](https://www.wpc.ncep.noaa.gov/discussions/pmdepdbody.txt), issued Sunday November 15 at 1:53 PM EST and valid Wednesday November 18 through Sunday November 22, flags one dominant hazard: an atmospheric river targeting California. The discussion calls for moderate to heavy rainfall along the coast and into interior California Wednesday into Thursday, with heavy snow in the Sierra, parts of Utah, and the San Juan Mountains. A flooding risk accompanies the rainfall. The system then spreads precipitation eastward across the Intermountain West and eventually into the Plains late in the week, with snow possible on the northern flank of the associated surface low and high winds expected across the Plains Friday into Saturday. The extended period also carries uncertainty about a low pressure system that may develop east of Florida and track northward over the weekend, potentially bringing rain to the Eastern Seaboard — model spread on that scenario remains wide as of the discussion's issue time.

**Denver area** shows a brief warm spell Monday and Tuesday with highs of 62°F and 70°F respectively before a cooldown to the mid-to-upper 50s midweek. Light precipitation probability increases toward the weekend, with a small drizzle signal Saturday (0.01 inches, 20% probability).

**San Francisco area** is the week's most active domestic forecast. Rain arrives Monday (0.22 inches, 87% probability), a brief dry window Tuesday, then the atmospheric river moves in Wednesday through Thursday — 1.10 inches and 1.03 inches respectively at the point forecast, with probabilities of 99% and 90%. Rain chances persist through the weekend, with Saturday showing 0.69 inches at 52% probability.

---

The week ahead splits cleanly along a continental divide that runs closer to the Sierra Nevada than the Rockies: the East gets the high-pressure dividend — dry air, above-normal temperatures, and a Thanksgiving-week setup that looks cooperative — while California takes the season's most significant rain and snow event to date, with flooding a real concern for coastal watersheds still carrying the memory of a dry October. The Plains uncertainty, particularly the potential Gulf low and its track, is the variable worth watching as the extended period unfolds.
